Inconsistency Detection of Heterogeneous Process Engineering Artifacts

TUM Institute of Automation and Information Systems

Research Assistant · Munich, DE · 11.2024 – 03.2026

As a research assistant at TUM’s Institute of Automation and Information Systems (AIS), I worked on the IEV4PI project, supporting information tracking and inconsistency detection across heterogeneous engineering artifacts. My work covered use-case analysis, workflow and ontology modelling, and the development of tools for converting and querying engineering information.

I analysed engineering use cases and modelling requirements through literature and industry standards, then formalised industrial engineering workflows in BPMN. These process models provided the context for designing ontologies to represent how engineering information is created, exchanged, and used throughout a business process. The work involved connecting a process-oriented view of engineering activities with a semantic representation of the information involved.

Alongside the modelling work, I supported the implementation effort. I helped improve an existing parser for converting XMI/XML exports into OWL ontologies, helping bring exported model information into a form that could be queried. I also assisted with implementing query functionality using W3C technologies to support information tracking across the represented engineering artifacts. This work connected the analysis of engineering practices with formal models and software tools for inspecting their information relationships.

Part of this work contributed to our IECON 2026 paper:

A Modular Ontology Design for Attribute-Level Traceability Across Engineering Documents in Process Engineering

Y. Wang, C. Shen, J. Alterbaum, B. Chen, T. Kleinert, B. Vogel-Heuser

IECON 2026 Accepted

Abstract—Engineering traceability in process engineering projects is hindered by heterogeneous document structures, discipline-specific semantics, versioned artifacts, and distributed organizational responsibilities. This paper presents the Provenance-integrated Rule-based Information Semantics Model (PRISM), a modular ontology design that provides a semantic basis for attribute-level traceability across engineering documents by separating process execution and information semantics, document concretizations, and project context into controlled yet connected modules. The design emphasizes interface modules that couple the semantic layers without collapsing their local modeling independence, thereby supporting extensibility and governance-aware integration. To illustrate the operational applicability of the design, PRISM is implemented as a modular ontology in the Description Logic profile of the Web Ontology Language 2 (OWL 2 DL) with externalized alignment configuration and executable trace queries over versioned project data. The successful realization of 18 scenarios within an anonymized industrial control valve use case demonstrates that the proposed design supports cross-document and cross-version trace retrieval while preserving modular structure and local representations. An expert evaluation with process engineering stakeholders indicates PRISM’s relevance as a complementary semantic traceability layer for brownfield, collaborative, and document-driven settings, while identifying data access, data quality, and interface integration as key adoption challenges.
